## 1. Diagnostic sequence (ordered)

This is the walk as actually performed on screen-shares, not an idealised version. Steps 1–3 happen for every account every day; steps 4–10 fire only when a step above them looks wrong.

### Step 0 — Establish the goal, and the *owner's* goal, before any metric

The entry point is not a metric. It is "what is this person trying to do this month".

> "when you go through the accounts every day, think of the owner's perspective first and then from a sort of like a marketing goals point of view." — Daniel, Nina & Daniel bi-weekly, 2026-06-26

> "on one hand there is the ROAS target for everyone, but then on the other is the growth or profitability. So for example, for Stella now it's about capital efficiency while she's managing cash, whilst we also need to sort of capitalize on the good weather." — Daniel, same call

For a new account this is an explicit question, and it is the first section of the codified audit:

> "This will come out of us asking them what their actual goal is because like during sign up we'll see what event they are optimizing for mostly and then we'll ask them. Well, you're optimizing for leads. What's your CPL like? You're optimizing for purchases. Do you have like a CPA goal or a ROAS goal?" — Daniel, 2026-06-26

> "we can start when we are introducing a new client with like, what are the actual goals so like what are we measuring against" — Daniel, Creative Strategy & Performance weekly, 2026-06-17

### Step 1 — Day-level P&L in Triple Whale, *before* Ads Manager

For ecom the first screen is not Meta. It is Triple Whale plugged into Shopify, read day by day.

> "Triple Whale is plugged into Shopify and it has all the cost data for all the clients. And there we can break down profits and profitability on a day to day basis… on the right you see the ROAS in Triple Whale, so it's all of the revenue divided by all of the ad spend and if there is a three something next to it usually Alex is happy whilst profit is already there." — Daniel, Creative Strategy & Performance weekly, 2026-06-17

Then the column that actually drives the decision — **Contribution Euros**, which is Triple Whale net profit with fixed OPEX added back:

> "on the very right side, right here we have a column called Contribution Euros and this is contribution after ads before fixed opex. So it's basically the triple whale net profit plus the opex." — Daniel, Teethlovers Weekly, 2026-07-23

> "we have in the funnel view a column called Contribution Euros. This is where we're trying to figure out how much contribution margin were actually generated through ads and then how much company OPEX impacts profitability. The logic here is to figure out whether spending more on ads would have made the day more profitable by offsetting the opex." — Daniel, bi-weekly, 2026-07-24

Daniel's stated ambition is to replace ROAS with profit at the top of the dashboard: *"rather than tracking ROAS here, for example, in here we should say profit."* (bi-weekly, 2026-07-24). Nina wants the scope widened beyond Meta: *"maybe should be like not the trip full whale view, but their whole like revenue and spend... this is not just meta ads, because that view is now just meta ads."*

### Step 2 — Trailing 7-day headline vs target

> "Can we look at the last seven days? **That's always what we're reporting in our meeting.**" — Vanessa, Account Management Weekly, 2026-07-23

Client-facing calls open on goals, always, before any media-buying detail:

> "So, as always, goals check in first." — Vanessa, Brain.fm, 2026-07-21
> "So our first order break even Target, Ross, is 3x in triple whale. We're consistently above this number." — Vanessa, Teethlovers, 2026-07-23

Note Daniel's default is 30 days and Vanessa pulls him back to 7. Both are in use (see *Time windows*).

### Step 3 — Their own funnel report, day by day. The column order *is* the diagnostic.

`spend → CPM → CTR → unique outbound CTR → PDP view rate → add-to-cart rate → checkout rate → purchases → ROAS → AOV → frequency` (+ population-weighted German temperature and cloud cover, for Laori only).

Read as good-days-vs-bad-days rather than as a level:

> "just generally on the bad days in the last 14 days, I see that the PDP view rate is significantly lower compared to the good days" — Daniel, bi-weekly, 2026-06-26

> "another thing about the good days, the AOV is massively higher than on the bad ones." — Daniel, same call

**Critical normalisation detail (do not get this wrong in Ada):** PDP view rate is normalised against *unique outbound clicks*, so it can and does exceed 100%. Add-to-cart rate is on a different base. This is why the two appear to move inversely.

> "PDP [rate] is against unique outbound clicks." — Daniel, bi-weekly, 2026-05-11 `(spk-mapped)`
> "it's imbalanced. Like you see when this is good, this is bad. When this is bad, this is good." — Daniel, same call

### Step 4 — Campaign level: where did the spend *move*?

This is their signature entry question, and the one they most want automated.

> "Is there a new ad set that is all of a sudden getting more spend?" — Daniel, bi-weekly, 2026-07-24

> "keep track of the relative share of ad spend an ad set or ad is getting within a campaign or account or ad set, depending on the structure, and then alerting us if there are major movements. Like for example, in the Brain FM account, 3994 all of a sudden took off in the bid cap." — Daniel, same call

Spend share is also treated as Meta's own verdict on creative quality — including as the measure of whether *their* creative is winning against a competing supplier's:

> "at least he's not getting the top spend… That would have been a crisis meeting, guys. This would have been like, how come someone else is outperforming us?" — Daniel, Creative Strategy weekly, 2026-06-22

### Step 5 — Read spend and frequency as a **pair**

> "so you decrease the bit caps. But it's spent even more than the day before." … "And At a lower frequency, which is great." … "So spent going up whilst frequency going down is great. So that means that we're able to reach new audiences." — Daniel, bi-weekly, 2026-07-24

The full doctrine, taught aloud:

> "we're also looking at frequency as an indicator of audience. So when we, for example, when we have a top spending ad set at the very, very top, and even though it doesn't have the best performance, we still sometimes we keep it running to populate the top of the funnel." — Daniel, Creative Strategy weekly, 2026-06-22

> "The lower the frequency, the easier. So basically it's an indicator that meta, rather than trying to show the same ad to the same people, it's going broad, it's finding new audience to then later retarget. But these ones, they have an incredibly high frequency." — Daniel, same call

> "A frequency of 1.5 means that we've reached 100 people 150 times today, which means that every second person, so 50 people of those have seen this ad twice. And then we are shoving this ad down people's throats." — Daniel, same call

> "usually when we see accounts where the top performing ad has a daily frequency of say 1.2, which means that just every fifth person sees the ad twice a day means that Meta is struggling to find an audience." — Daniel, Brain.fm, 2026-07-21

Diagnostic check they run explicitly: *"Last seven days, is our top spending ad set the lowest frequency one?"* (Daniel, 2026-05-11 `(spk-mapped)`).

### Step 6 — Spend/performance mismatch as the trigger to drill

> "the hypothesis here would be that why, so why is Meta spending more money here than it is here, even though these ones have much better performance? So then the theory is that probably the frequency of those is a lot less." — Daniel, Creative Strategy weekly, 2026-06-22

Then: *"let's compare the Highest performing one here, 703 against this one. And then we're going into the daily breakdown of trends and then we, rather than going for cost per result, we're going into frequency."*

### Step 7 — Ad level, sorted by amount spent, last 7 or 14. Then diff winners against losers.

> "What I'm missing here now is also what's the difference in the funnel of these underperforming ads? Why are they so expensive compared to the others?" → "here there is lower conversion rate on those. So conversion rate seems to be the main thing." — Daniel, bi-weekly, 2026-06-22

The pairwise ad walk, in order: spend → ROAS → hook rate → hold rate → CTR → CPC → relevance → age/gender impression *and purchase* breakdowns.

> "let's have a look at two ads. So let's go with the Vivi authority drops, the airport ads just to diagnose a little bit. Okay, let's go into the Vivi ads first, see why they are not working." — Daniel, 2026-05-11 `(spk-mapped)`

### Step 8 — Breakdowns are **mandatory** before any kill decision

This is taught as a rule, not a habit:

> "when we're doing an analysis like this, we also need... looking into the breakdowns because otherwise we won't really understand what is happening. So for example, was it really, for example a... Was it a country thing?" … "basically when doing an analysis like this, try also doing these type of breakdowns because this will help us understand what the cause is. **And if we don't understand the cause, then it's difficult for us to make decisions like that.**" — Daniel, bi-weekly, 2026-06-22

Order used: **country → placement → age → device.** Worked example from that call: *"all of the spend came from Thailand"* → *"majority of it is Facebook in feed. Barely any audience network, which is great but still really high frequency"* → *"18 to 24 in Thailand is what Meta decided to spend on"* → *"Android is fine. IOS expensive"*.

When outbound CTR is high but conversions are absent, first suspicion is **placement junk** — he names *rewarded video* and *audience network* specifically (2026-05-11 `(spk-mapped)`).

### Step 9 — Click the destination URL. In incognito.

> "the next piece of investigation would be... where are we actually leading? And rather than taking it at face value we'd be looking into. Okay, is it actually the case that we're sending this one to the right page? So let's see. So we go into this ad set, we take the top spending one and then we look at the link and then we actually click on the link in an incognito." — Daniel, bi-weekly, 2026-06-22

### Step 10 — Read the comments on the ad

> "Is it comments on it?" — Daniel, bi-weekly, 2026-07-24 (investigating a surprise winner)
> "Are there maybe some negative comments? ... **By the way this should also be an ADA skill to actually go and check.**" — Daniel, bi-weekly, 2026-06-01

### Step 11 — Pixel / dataset health, when the numbers stop making sense

Triggered by impossible values, not on a schedule:

- **Negative checkout-abandonment = broken data.** *"the checkout abandonment rate is... This is weird. Negative. So we have more purchases than checkouts. This makes zero sense."* (Daniel, 2026-06-01)
- **Impossible aggregate = broken data.** *"If we do results from 110 campaigns here we have 199% PDP view rate. But then if we look at the entire like the campaigns individually, none of them is higher than 190. This makes no sense."* (Daniel, 2026-06-01)
- **Zero where zero is impossible.** *"Zero. What? No, but this is not correct."* (Daniel, 2026-06-26)
- On rejections: check dataset flags, then Business Settings → Data sources → **brand safety domains**. Found: *"we only have access to one domain, which is Irvy"* — i.e. not the domain that mattered. Nina: *"Lovely. The one we need the most."* (2026-06-26)
- **Link-click → landing-page-view collapse is the single highest-value pixel tripwire.**
  > Daniel: "**17 Landing page views from 633 links.**"
  > Steven Roberts: "This is the campaign where we're **not sending all of the pixel data**. That's why."
  > Daniel: "Oh, okay... **But it's good that it caught it though.**" — 2026-06-23

  **Rule: an LPV/link-click collapse means suspect instrumentation before creative or landing page.** Unambiguous, and it silently destroys optimisation.
- **Malformed click identifiers.** *"I got a couple of notifications in Ads Manager, one related to the leads event and the— and the purchase event. Um, where, uh, basically **Dmytro has been sending the FB clipped rather than the FBC**."* (Daniel, 2026-07-21) — a truncated/malformed `fbc` parameter on the two events that matter most, degrading match quality.
- **EMQ (event match quality)** is a first-class audit line: *"where you are in terms of like your EMQ on the data set"*; *"Then EMQ on the data set. I think we'll be able to get this from the API… it has this new data quality API for the new data sets."* (2026-06-26)
- **Optimisation-event hygiene:** the audit checks *"whether they're optimizing for add to carts or landing page views"* rather than purchases (2026-06-26). Same class of error found on Google: *"The conversions that came in were add to carts, by the way. Not conversions, not sales… I just removed the primary add to cart conversion."* (2026-05-11 `(spk-mapped)`)
- **Isolation trick when a funnel report is polluted by server-side redirects:** filter the third-party funnel report to a **single ad ID via `utm_id`** and re-read. *"let me take the highest spending ad that we have and then we can look at it on one ad ID level"* → *"Okay, so here we have it. So it's not a creative thing. It's an actual funnel thing."* (Daniel, Brain.fm, 2026-05-27)

### Step 11b — Cross-account CPM check: is this you, or is it the auction?

> "I saw this message and I'm already checking with our— with our little AI helpers. We haven't, like, I personally haven't noticed anything, but I want to see what happened across your account that might have led to rising CPMs. Because **this is what I've seen is like we haven't seen a rise in CPMs across the board. So usually it indicates that there's been a shift in audience.**" — Daniel, 2026-06-23

**Stated inference rule: CPM up in one account but not across the portfolio ⇒ audience shift, not auction pressure.** Directly encodable, and a check no single-account tool can offer — Ada sees many accounts natively.

### Step 11c — The generic degradation chain (use when nothing else localises)

The cleanest statement of their chain reasoning:

> "So basically **CPM down means cheaper audience. Frequency up means less new audiences. CTR down means people clicking less, exploring the product less, exploring products less. And once they hit the PDP, the add to cart rate is also less.**" — Daniel, Ninepine, 2026-05-12

Kousha Torabi's label for the pattern: *"It sounds like an audio quality traffic... it's a **traffic quality** issue."*

**Diagnostic rule: when every downstream rate degrades simultaneously while CPM improves, the problem is who is being bought — not the creative and not the landing page.**

### Step 12 — Rule out non-paid traffic before accepting blame

> "I literally just opened sessions coming to the website by source, and then their direct none in Shopify went from like almost 3,000 sessions to 1,000 sessions." → Daniel: "Oh, is it... Is it SEO?" → Nina: "Exactly. And that's what I told him. I was like, **meta is actually up from the last sale, but you have a problem somewhere else**." → Daniel: "Great analysis." — bi-weekly, 2026-07-20 (Press London)

> "Such crops [drops] are usually SEO related." — Daniel, same call

### The codified full audit ("the magic audit") — Ada's target section order

Daniel dictated this to Nina on 2026-06-26. It is the most explicit statement of account-opening methodology in the corpus.

1. Competitive benchmarks — *"where you are in terms of like your EMQ on the data set, hook rate on the videos funnel"* + *"benchmarks like budget efficiency"*
2. *"First ads by spend in ROAS"*
3. **Spend concentration** — *"Like top three ads doing 71% of spend for Brain FM… This would have been 90% in one app."*
4. Targeting audit — *"How much goes to broad lookalike, interest retargeting, then breakdown by placements… You see percentage of spend in ROAS, Breakdown by age and by country."*
5. *"Breakdown by product category and ROAS"* — *"By URL, but in a smart way, grouping them into groups."*
6. EMQ on the dataset (via the data-quality API)
7. Funnel + checkout, then **landing page breakdown**
8. *"Then optimization whether they're optimizing for add to carts or landing page views."*
9. Creative analysis — *"what are the things that the best ads have in common. Then angles, which ones have the best performance and share of spend."*
10. **Ad fatigue predictor** — with his own caveat: *"I think it's good in theory, but now I need to check whether we're actually able to pull this off in practice"*
11. Share of ad spend by ad age (*"breakdown of months and the share of ad spend depending on how old the ads actually were"*)
12. *"a relative chart of where you are with your hook rate and hold rate the account"*
13. CPM over time, day of week, hour by ROAS, next steps

Headline framing of the output: *"34K a month. That you'd get back."*

**Their working definition of "not enough data" is spend volume behind the number, not days elapsed.** The 3-day exchange above is the clearest case: the number was dismissed because *"It spent not even €20"*, not because three days is too short. **Statistical significance never comes up as a concept in any of the 26 transcripts** — no confidence, no power, no significance thresholds. The substitutes are minimum-spend floors, spend-weighted reads, and multi-window agreement.

**Day-of-week is a first-class signal, not noise.**
> "it's funny, weird that it's always like Tuesday, Wednesday, sometimes even Thursday that are not good for Lowry… if you look at the whole week, it's usually Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday. And then before it's fine, after it's fine or at least it gets better." — Nina, 2026-06-26
> "Saturdays are really bad for them." / Daniel: "And Sundays are usually good... it's because of the newsletter." — bi-weekly, 2026-07-24

**Meta's day is not their day.** *"I think it's the cleaning up because yesterday for meta was still today for us."* (Nina, 07-24). Also an upload-lag caveat: *"Maybe look at not yesterday and today, but the day before and yesterday… But it's the time difference."* (Nina, 06-26)

**Weather is an explicit modelled covariate for Laori** (summer drinks brand), built into the report as population-weighted German temperature + cloud cover + sunshine hours.
> "you can pretty much trace temperature and return on ad spend. Like if temperature rises, ROAS rises, if temperature drops, ROAS drops." — Daniel, 2026-06-17

Nina notes the limit: *"it's weird because like the cloud percentage is not really affecting ROAS that much."*

## 3. Decision rules

### Scale

- **The ecom scale trigger is positive contribution margin, not ROAS.** *"as long as we are positive on the contribution margin, I think we should be spending more on meta to become more profitable."* (Daniel, Teethlovers, 2026-07-23)
- **Increment: ~20%/day, laddered over about a week, to a named destination number.** Not open-ended.
- **Scaling is framed to the client as reversible.** *"as fast as we're ramping the budget up, we can also ramp it back down. So it's not going to be like we're going to ramp it and then it's there for three months."* (Daniel, 2026-07-23)
- **Scaling is framed as a hypothesis test.** *"the entire idea of that is to become profitable, to become consistently profitable. Not like breaking even, but profitable. And we need to validate this hypothesis of whether this will hold strong."* (Daniel, 2026-07-23)
- **On bid-cap campaigns you scale the BID, not the budget.** *"just pushing more spend towards it does not work as it would on a normal campaign, but we do need to increase bids."* (Nina, Brain.fm, 2026-07-14)
- **Scale on weekend-proof performance.** *"you spent 13,000 on Saturday with 38 cost per purchase… So there is room to push if a performance like that is possible during the weekend."* (Daniel, bi-weekly, 2026-07-20)
- **Scale to cover fixed OPEX even at lower ROAS.** The biggest doctrinal shift in the corpus: *"even here, for example, like here we had like a lower Roas but because we spent more... on these days we got away… one thing that we need to do is keep also spend high enough so that a day like that could have been profitable."* (Daniel, bi-weekly, 2026-06-22)
- **Under-spending is an error with a measurable revenue cost.** *"Ever since June 8th we've dropped spend so much so that yes, profits I think went up a little bit, but overall the revenue has decreased."* (Daniel, 2026-06-22)
- **Deliberately scale into good days.** *"on these days, by the way, we need to figure out how to crank the machine, how to increase spend a lot more."* (Daniel, 2026-06-22). For weather-dependent accounts this is asymmetric: *"our job is to scale fast enough on the warm days when the ROAS goes up and capture as much profit as we can, whilst minimizing losses on the bad days."* (Daniel, 2026-06-17)
- **Confirm with blended before pushing.** *"What does Triple Whale say? Because if Meta was under reporting"* → *"but still, I mean it means that we can push"* → *"Yeah, let's push spend."* (2026-06-26)
- **Accept CPA drift while scaling if the channel still beats the alternative.** *"I know you guys pushed a little bit and the cost per result went up. That's okay. That's okay. Because it's still been more efficient than Google."* (Adam de Jong, 2026-06-23)
- **Raw scale is always on the list.** *"the one thing that I haven't mentioned at all is just ranking spend up, which is still the most straightforward way of growing and just basically seeing where things start capping out and seeing where we hit diminishing returns."* (Daniel, 2026-07-21)
- **The agency's standing bias is that they under-scale, and hesitation is treated as the defect.** *"this is generally something that I've noticed with Nina that sometimes she hesitates with ad spend increases… Because this is making or breaking the business."* (Daniel, AM Weekly, 2026-06-24). Nina's counter-position is recorded under *Disagreements*.

### Pause / kill

- **Tighten before you kill** (bid caps). **Exclude before you kill** (geo): *"the next step for here rather than shutting them off it would be to exclude Thailand."* (Daniel, 2026-06-22)
- **Kill outright only to "stop the bleeding"** on runaway spend, or when a campaign is structurally too expensive: *"this campaign is getting too expensive. So frankly, maybe just kill it. This frees up more spend to be spent well elsewhere."* (Daniel, 2026-06-01)
- **Kill the non-converting ad inside a working ad set** so the set's blended CPA improves: *"I paused the monologue, the yapper one because it… didn't convert and the other ones got all the conversion."* (Adam de Jong, 2026-06-23)
- **Never turn off a performer to fix an internal mistake.** *"we as internally need to be careful because I'm not turning off an ad that is doing well just because we fucked up."* (Nina, 2026-06-01)
- **Don't kill an ad set carrying a live experiment.** *"we aren't touching the student ad set just yet. Like, this one we keep running because there's an amplitude test."* (Daniel, 2026-05-29)
- **Check frequency and new-customer contribution before killing a suspect ad set.** *"Let's have a look at frequency. Has it really been that good to us? Let's see if the frequency has been low. It has been low. So it is driving a lot of new customers in high hope [hook] rates."* → kept. (2026-06-01)
- **Debug before you kill — isolate the variable.** The single cleanest statement of the method: *"I also think that it may be a creative thing because we don't have creative parity between our main CBO and the bit caps… So what if we take like rather than killing it entirely, taking our top three performing ad sets and putting them into the remarketing and seeing how they would perform in there. So that we can at least sort of like remove the variable of like, is it the technology or is it the creative and run that for a couple of days."* (Daniel, Brain.fm, 2026-07-21) — **and hold the budget constant while debugging:** *"I just leave it where it is. Like just like for the sake of reducing variables."*
- **Don't mix variables in a test.** *"if we do ASC target ROAS. We are sort of mixing variables here."* → resolution: *"let's do one regular target ROAS campaign and then one ASC campaign."* (2026-05-11 `(spk-mapped)`)
- **Never briefly pause ads to fix tracking.** Stated as a rule-by-negative-example about a client's in-house buyer: *"she said that they were pausing ads briefly just to fix tracking and it seems they did. So apparently, which is so stupid like the first thing that Meta tells you… don't briefly turn off ads."* (Top client prep, 2026-07-20 — *speaker attribution unreliable, broken diarization*)
- **Retargeting gets a shorter leash than prospecting.** *"especially for a remarketing campaign… then there should have been slower."* (Nina, 2026-07-21). Historic pause reason: *"It was performing worse than Prospecting and we were like, what's the point?"* (Adam de Jong, 2026-07-14)
- **Protect the client's downside proactively.** *"us as media buyers, we then need to be on top of this. And if, for example, we see that things aren't performing, rather than keeping them running, being proactive about this pinging John saying like, hey John, this isn't working. We need to protect your downside."* (Daniel, 2026-06-26). Precedent: *"after a few days we were losing money on it, so we paused it."*
- **Bulk hygiene:** delete zero-spend/near-zero-impression ads to reclaim the **200-ads-per-campaign** limit. *"what I've done is I sorted by spend. And I killed some of these... The ones that have never had any spend in them."* (Daniel, 2026-05-29 — 109 ads selected, "121 Ads" deleted from Laori)

### Creative: kill vs give it more time

**The house testing system, stated in full.** This is the most complete statement of the method anywhere in 33 transcripts. It happens to be spoken on a leadgen call (Daniel explaining AOT's system to Audibene's in-house buyer), but it is the house system and applies to the ecom accounts too:

> "**We upload ads every Monday.** Every Monday, we put them into our **testing and scaling CBO** that basically has— **we are protecting our downside, but then the upside is uncapped.** So we— we upload a new ad set, then, uh, they get a **minimum spend of X**, and then by the— by the time the week finishes, which is, well, for us, we don't count the weekends because it's a bid, like, business-related product, so we count Monday through Friday. **Between Monday and Friday, we want to get 10 times target CPA in spend across the ad set. And then on Friday, we make the decision, do we want to keep running it or not.**"
>
> "If by— by the time Friday comes, **it hasn't scaled, then we— then we remove the cap, and then we let it just fizzle out and compete.** If, uh, if it starts fizzling out and, like, not really doing anything, then we put it in our **bid cap graveyard, where ads go to die**, and we still sort of give them, I mean, like, a little bit of a chance without— without really promoting our main goal."
>
> "We always know that **Mondays are upload days, Fridays are always analysis days.** And we— we have sort of **parity across all the creative analyses.**"
> — Daniel Bulygin, Audibene, 2026-07-21

Decoded:
1. **Upload day = Monday. Decision day = Friday. Never in between.**
2. **"Enough data" = 10 × target CPA in ad-set spend**, accumulated Mon–Fri (weekends excluded for business-shaped products).
3. Downside protected by a **minimum spend floor**; upside **uncapped** — the CBO may overspend a winner.
4. Not scaled by Friday ⇒ **remove the floor**, let it compete unaided. **Do not kill.**
5. Still fizzling ⇒ demote to the **bid-cap graveyard**, a strict-cap campaign. **Still do not kill.**
6. **Kill is rare. Demotion is the default.**

**This rule is live, not aspirational.** It was first worked out aloud on 2026-05-11, in response to Nina asking for a stopping rule (*"can we just make KPIs and say like, okay, when we hit this, we will say it's enough of testing"*), with Daniel deriving it: *"because our targets cost per purchase is about 35 bucks. So what if we say 10 times target CPA, we spend 350 bucks on an ad and then we make a decision."* Ten weeks later he describes it to a client as the standing system.

The softer ecom-side statement of the same cadence, with an explicit extension clause:

> "we publish them on Mondays, then we let them run for a week or two. And then either on the first Friday we are making a decision, do we kill it or not? **And if it's not really clear, then we keep it running for one more week.**" — Daniel, Creative Strategy weekly, 2026-06-17

**Honest admission from the same 07-21 call:** *"we have a couple of winners, and then... it's hard for us to find ads that are better. And just recently we found 2 more ads, which we're very proud of."*

6. **Frequency thresholds: is the exhaustion line 1.2 or 1.5?** 1.2 was called "struggling to find an audience" (07-21), 1.5 was called bottom-of-funnel behaviour (06-22). And should it be evaluated on the top-spending ad, the ad set, or the account?
7. **Hook-rate benchmark: 30% globally, or per account?** 30% is quoted as both "our benchmark" and "the Brain.fm account average". Statics-animated-to-video have their own floor (7–10%). Ada should probably compare to the account's own trailing distribution — confirm.
8. **How should Ada handle low-spend accounts?** Their own rule is spend-volume-based, not day-based, but no floor was ever stated except €50/day min spend and 10× target CPA. For a €30/day Guard account, what is "enough data"?
9. **Scale increment: 20%/day appears for cost caps, bid caps, and budgets alike.** Is 20% the universal step, and what is the stop condition other than "until they bottom out"?

**On things Ada should probably not say:**

10. **Confirm the hook-rate stance.** Daniel's 2026-07-08 position is that hook/hold/CTR are what non-performance operators look at *instead of* purchases, and reporting them is flattery *"not grounded in… performance reality"*. 11. **Attribution-window settings never came up in 33 transcripts.** Is that because the accounts are all on 7-day-click/1-day-view defaults and it genuinely doesn't matter, or because nobody's checking? For Guard accounts this is a plausible silent killer.

So usually it indicates that there's been a shift in audience."* Should Ada run this natively across all connected accounts and tell owners "this is you, not the auction" / "this is the auction, not you"? No single-account tool can do it, and it directly serves the Guard promise of suppressing false alarms.
