## How Ada applies this

1. This is the house METHOD, taken verbatim from recorded calls. Follow its sequence and its reasoning; never read it out at a customer as a rulebook, and never cite it as an authority.
2. Every number in here was measured on the account it names. Check it against THIS account's own data and its own goal bands before you use it, and say which reference you are comparing against.
3. Never name another account, another client, or their numbers to a customer. The method travels between accounts; the accounts it came from do not.
4. Where this playbook and the customer's own settled decisions disagree, their decision wins, and you say plainly which rule you are setting aside and why.

## 1. Diagnostic sequence (ordered)

### Step 0 — Goal check-in, always first, before any data

> "Okay, so. Very first, **as always, starting with the goals check-in**, um, I wanted to ask if our target of CPA of 139 is still current. Um, we're hovering a little bit above that consistently, so I wanted to check in." — Daniel Bulygin, Audibene, 2026-07-21

This is identical on the ecom side (*"So, as always, goals check in first"* — Vanessa, Brain.fm, 2026-07-21). The house pattern is: **confirm the target is still the target before reporting against it.** In this case it wasn't — the 139 turned out to be a January number nobody had revised.

For a new account the goal is elicited explicitly, and it is the first section of the codified audit:

> "This will come out of us asking them what their actual goal is because like during sign up we'll see what event they are optimizing for mostly and then we'll ask them. Well, **you're optimizing for leads. What's your CPL like?** You're optimizing for purchases. Do you have like a CPA goal or a ROAS goal?" — Daniel, bi-weekly, 2026-06-26

And the framing that comes before even that:

> "when you go through the accounts every day, **think of the owner's perspective first** and then from a sort of like a marketing goals point of view." — Daniel, bi-weekly, 2026-06-26

### Step 1 — Locate the break by funnel stage: cost side → CR1 → CR2

This is **the** leadgen-specific move, and it is stated as a question Daniel asks before touching anything:

> "Do we know— do we know, like, **at which stage of the funnel things start breaking down? Is it that, uh, CR1 or CR2 is lower**, or where are you seeing it?" — Daniel, Audibene, 2026-07-21

Steven: *"In general, it's— it's right higher than that. It's all on the marketing cost side, so it's not so much the other side."* Daniel: *"Oh, so CPM's going up?"*

Stage definitions as they use them:
- **CR1** = ad click / traffic → lead
- **CR2** = lead → qualified / appointment / sale

Worked attribution from the 2026-06-23 call: *"But this looks like a... So that's a **CR1 problem**. CR1 problem that may have stemmed from..."* and later, on high CPA: *"I mean a lot. A lot of it is **CR2**."*

### Step 2 — Cross-account check, to separate platform-wide from account-specific

> "I saw this message and I'm already checking with our— with our little AI helpers. We haven't, like, I personally haven't noticed anything, but I want to see what happened across your account that might have led to rising CPMs. Because **this is what I've seen is like we haven't seen a rise in CPMs across the board. So usually it indicates that there's been a shift in audience.**" — Daniel, Audibene, 2026-06-23

**Stated inference rule: CPM up in one account but not across the portfolio ⇒ audience shift, not auction pressure.** This is directly encodable — Ada sees many accounts and can run this check natively.

### Step 3 — Isolate the anomalous day, then the campaign, then the ad

Ada's own output, read aloud by Daniel on 2026-06-23:

> "Yes, **Saturday is a real anomaly. CPL spiked.** 2 recently launched here **three CBO campaigns that ate 57% of the day spend and returned almost no leads.**"

Then re-slice the isolated entity by day:

> "If we isolate this one specifically and then we go into **breakdown by day**, it's looking good... Like now the last few days is getting better. So it started with retargeting a little bit more and now it's starting to find a new audience." — Daniel, Ninepine, 2026-05-05

### Step 4 — Check the link-click → landing-page-view gap as a pixel tripwire

> Daniel: "**17 Landing page views from 633 links.**"
> Steven: "This is the campaign where we're **not sending all of the pixel data**. That's why."
> Daniel: "Oh, okay... **But it's good that it caught it though.**"
> — Audibene, 2026-06-23

**Rule: an LPV/link-click collapse means suspect instrumentation before creative or landing page.** For a leadgen account this is the highest-value single check Ada can run, because it is unambiguous and it silently destroys optimisation.

### Step 5 — Lead disposition and lead quality (the part that has nothing to do with Meta)

Named states in the Audibene CRM, all of which are CR2 killers:
- **"Not reached"** — call didn't connect. Daniel: *"So Friday also I usually don't see this many not reached."* Steven: *"we noticed that as a trend last week that a lot of stuff was going into— not reached."*
- **"Out-of-closing" / "out-of-closed"** — leads removed from the closeable pool by business rule
- **"Net leads"** — leads minus out-of-closed
- **Lead score** — computed client-side
- **"First Care Share"** — a lead-quality metric where *"Grün ist niedrig, heißt, ist gut"* (green = low = good)
- **"Degree of Suffering" / Behinderungsgrad**, **"First Care without Prescription"** — lead-quality dimensions and segments

Daniel reads these as a heatmap over 90 days and looks for the contradiction:

> "Also im Endeffekt First Care Share. Grün ist niedrig, heißt, ist gut. Und da sehen wir eigentlich, und das ist jetzt auf die letzten 90 Tage verteilt, sieht es ja eigentlich hier in den letzten paar Wochen relativ grün aus. Also wir haben viel Repeat, aber trotzdem ist halt die CO2 [CR2]... hier ist ja richtig, richtig niedrig." — Daniel, Audibene SEA, 2026-06-01
> *(...it looks relatively green over the last few weeks. So we have a lot of repeat, but CR2 is still really, really low.)*

**The diagnostic he is running: lead-quality signals green + CR2 at a floor = the problem is downstream of lead quality.** Michelle's independent check agreed: *"Ich hatte da auch keine Ausreißer gesehen in seinen Lead-Scores, die er da ausrechnet oder anderen Signalen."* (I saw no outliers in the lead scores or other signals either.)

The honest end of that thread: *"Und dazwischen die Lücke, da sind wir eigentlich genauso schlau wie ihr."* (As for the gap in between, we're exactly as clueless as you are.)

### Step 6 — Neutralise the business-rule confound before blaming media

Daniel's methodological proposal, and it is the sharpest idea in the leadgen material:

> "Wir können ja mal eigentlich mal vergleichen und das mal **agnostisch machen von den Out-of-Closing-Rules, indem wir den CO2 [CR2] berechnen auf Net-Leads anstelle von Leads**. Weil dann würde man ja eigentlich das direkt so angeglichen bekommen." — Daniel, Audibene SEA, 2026-06-01
> *(Let's make it agnostic of the out-of-closing rules by calculating CR2 on net leads instead of leads. Then you'd get it directly comparable.)*

The client had already run the counterfactual: *"wir wissen ja auch, welche Leads wir warum geschlossen haben. Und da haben wir uns ja angeschaut, welche haben wir jetzt out-of-closed und wie wäre die Conversion gewesen, wenn wir sie nicht out-of-closed hätten."* Result: **out-of-closing explains only 5% relative of the CR2 drop**, and Daniel pinned that down explicitly:

> Daniel: "Also 5 % oder 5 % Punkte?" — Michelle: "**5 %. 5 % Punkte wären zu dramatisch.**"

### Step 7 — Date the change point before hunting causes

> "also wir haben also **seit dem 9. Mai** ja eigentlich, so sagen wir, **seit dem 7. Mai**, würde ich mal sagen, ging es dann auf einmal runter." — Daniel, Audibene SEA, 2026-06-01

He names a date range for the break first, then looks for what changed on those dates. (Same move on the ecom side: *"something happened on the Friday here that led to redistribution of traffic and a loss of sales."*)

### Step 8 — Re-pull the data before believing an anomaly

> Daniel: "So basically here in this, it says that **no landing page has received 200,000 [SEK] of ad spend yesterday**. So this basically means that pretty much all of the..."
> Kousha: "**something became unknown and reclassified as unknown**."
> Daniel: "**I need to repull the data.** Maybe, but it's— **it's unusual that specifically on the one day when the ROAS has been shit that we're also seeing something like this.**"
> — Ninepine, 2026-05-12

**Rule: a metric collapse that coincides exactly with a classification or attribution anomaly is a suspected measurement artifact until the pipeline has been re-pulled.**

Same instinct on Meta numbers generally: *"Oh wait, hold on, the interest is the switch has a hook rate of fifty five percent. What the fuck? I don't get this… I'll need to revisit why that is and whether this data is correct."* (Daniel, 2026-06-17)

### Step 9 — The generic degradation chain (use when nothing else localises)

The single cleanest statement of their chain reasoning. Worded for ecom but it is the template:

> "So basically **CPM down means cheaper audience. Frequency up means less new audiences. CTR down means people clicking less, exploring the product less, exploring products less. And once they hit the PDP, the add to cart rate is also less.**" — Daniel, Ninepine, 2026-05-12

Kousha's label for the pattern: *"It sounds like an audio quality traffic... it's a **traffic quality** issue."*

**Diagnostic rule: when every downstream rate degrades simultaneously while CPM improves, the problem is who is being bought — not the creative and not the landing page.** For leadgen the chain terminates at form-start / lead instead of PDP / ATC.

### Step 10 — Breakdowns are mandatory before any kill decision

> "when we're doing an analysis like this, we also need... looking into the breakdowns because otherwise we won't really understand what is happening. So for example, was it really, for example a... Was it a country thing?" … "**And if we don't understand the cause, then it's difficult for us to make decisions like that.**" — Daniel, bi-weekly, 2026-06-22

Order used: **country → placement → age → device.**

When outbound CTR is high but conversions are absent, first suspicion is **placement junk** — *rewarded video* and *audience network* named specifically (2026-05-11 `(spk-mapped)`).

### Step 11 — Rule out non-paid and non-media causes

- **Weather.** *"it correlates pretty significantly with the sharp temperature increases in Germany. We have another client where we literally have the temperature in here as another column."* (Daniel, 2026-06-23). Applied to leadgen specifically as a CR2 hypothesis: heat wave → older leads don't answer the phone. Daniel: *"Is it maybe also related to heat and just like older people not tolerating heat so well?"* Steven, dryly: *"They don't want to answer the phone because it's too hot."* then *"we usually imagine that people are busy doing things rather than too hot to talk to us."*
- **Public holidays, and the week after.** *"man hat wirklich gesehen, dass es halt die Folgewoche wirklich runtergezogen hat und so ein bisschen einfach den Algo... wie so ein kleines Bein gestellt hat"* (you could really see it dragged the following week down and tripped up the algo) — Aaron Pammer, 2026-06-01. Method for pre-empting: *"Wie das letztes Jahr war, right, in den Accounts... Letztes Jahr und dann im Zapfen schon wieder so eine Adjustment machen wie bei den anderen Feiertagen"* (look at last year and pre-adjust).
- **Client's other channels.** Whether Meta's CPA is acceptable is partly a question about the alternatives — see thresholds.

### Step 12 — Read the comments on the ad

> "Are there maybe some negative comments? ... **By the way this should also be an ADA skill to actually go and check.**" — Daniel, bi-weekly, 2026-06-01

**The lead-to-qualification lag is real and quantified: ~2 weeks of backfill.**

> "wir haben ja drei Voice-Bots und einer ist ganz neu... die rufen im Prinzip Out-of-Close-Leads an, die kommen dann noch dazu. Also **das wird jetzt noch nachreifen**. Dieser Bot, der ging letzte Woche live, der wird First Care without Prescription vorqualifizieren und **die letzten zwei Wochen noch anrufen**. Das heißt, da kann auch noch was dazukommen." — Michelle Adeyemi, Audibene SEA, 2026-06-01
> *(...that will still ripen... it will still call the last two weeks. So more can still come in.)*

Her conclusion: *"wir erwarten tendenziell eher Rückenwind für die CR2"* (we expect tailwind for CR2). **Encodable rule: CR2 for the trailing 2 weeks is provisional and biased low. Never fire a CR2 alert on the last 14 days without saying so.** This is the single biggest structural difference from ecom, where the purchase is the terminal event and lands same-session.

**"Enough data" is defined in spend, not days, and not conversions.** See the 10× target CPA rule below. The failure mode it was invented to fix, in Steven's words:

> "when I'd start a new ad group just for testing, **it would get hardly anything... basically I'd get a couple hundred euros spend, it would be expensive, and I'd need to pause it.**" — Audibene, 2026-07-21

Also, magnitude can override small-n: *"it's not a lot of data, but it's like 600 pieces sold, so it shouldn't increase returns by 50%."* (Kousha, 2026-05-05)

**Statistical significance never comes up as a concept in any of the 33 transcripts** — no confidence, no power, no significance thresholds. The substitutes are spend floors, spend-weighted reads, and multi-window agreement.

## 3. Decision rules

### The AOT creative testing system — stated in full, verbatim

This is the most complete statement of the house method anywhere in 33 transcripts. Audibene, 2026-07-21, Daniel Bulygin:

> "**We upload ads every Monday.** Every Monday, we put them into our **testing and scaling CBO** that basically has— **we are protecting our downside, but then the upside is uncapped.** So we— we upload a new ad set, then, uh, they get a **minimum spend of X**, and then by the— by the time the week finishes, which is, well, for us, we don't count the weekends because it's a bid, like, business-related product, so we count Monday through Friday. **Between Monday and Friday, we want to get 10 times target CPA in spend across the ad set. And then on Friday, we make the decision, do we want to keep running it or not.**"

> "If by— by the time Friday comes, **it hasn't scaled, then we— then we remove the cap, and then we let it just fizzle out and compete.** If, uh, if it starts fizzling out and, like, not really doing anything, then we put it in our **bid cap graveyard, where ads go to die**, and we still sort of give them, I mean, like, a little bit of a chance without— without really promoting our main goal."

> Steven: "We have a very strict cap, I guess." Daniel: "**Yeah, yeah, a very strict cap.**"

> "We always know that **Mondays are upload days, Fridays are always analysis days.** And we— we have sort of **parity across all the— all the creative analyses.**"

**Decoded into rules:**

1. **Upload day = Monday. Decision day = Friday. Never in between.**
2. **"Enough data" = 10 × target CPA in ad-set spend**, accumulated Mon–Fri.
3. Downside protected by a **minimum spend floor**; upside **uncapped** (the CBO is allowed to overspend a winner).
4. Not scaled by Friday ⇒ **remove the floor**, let it compete unaided. **Do not kill.**
5. Still fizzling ⇒ demote to the **bid-cap graveyard**, a strict-cap campaign. **Still do not kill.**
6. **Kill is rare. Demotion is the default.**

Corroboration that this rule is genuinely live and not aspirational: it was first proposed on 2026-05-11 in a bi-weekly, as a response to Nina's request for a stopping rule (*"can we just make KPIs and say like, okay, when we hit this, we will say it's enough of testing"*), with Daniel working it out aloud: *"because our targets cost per purchase is about 35 bucks. So what if we say 10 times target CPA, we spend 350 bucks on an ad and then we make a decision."* Ten weeks later he describes it to a client as the standing system.

**Honest admission from the same call:** *"we have a couple of winners, and then... it's hard for us to find ads that are better. And just recently we found 2 more ads, which we're very proud of."*

Complementary cadence rule from the ecom side, same shape: *"we publish them on Mondays, then we let them run for a week or two. And then either on the first Friday we are making a decision, do we kill it or not? And if it's not really clear, then we keep it running for one more week."* (Daniel, 2026-06-17)

### The competing view — daily steering vs weekly cadence (unresolved)

Steven is not adopting the Monday/Friday system, and says why:

> "one of the challenges with that for us is that we have all of this, um, **pressure from steering to shift things up and down all the time**... being able to launch new ads on a day where I need to— to scale. Um, so for example, tomorrow I need to scale up a bit. So it would help for me this afternoon to launch some new ads ready for that. Whereas yesterday we were scaling down a bit. So I didn't want to launch a whole load of new things yesterday."
> "**In general, I would like to be more structured, but it's very difficult when, uh, we're going up and down the whole time.**"

Daniel's counter-proposal, the **nuclear / battery split**:

> "there are certain power sources that are slow and steady and constant, especially in France. You have, let's say, like, **nuclear**, where you cannot scale nuclear up and down... But then you have **battery**, for example, that goes up and down... And we can have a structure in which we have sort of, like, the **nuclear campaign, where we know this is our bread and butter, this is, like, steady, this we don't touch, we know that this is isolated**, and then there's **batteries that can go up, down, left, right**, whatever you want them to."

Steven: *"For me, the stable one used to be the bid cap campaigns, and that was quite good for quite a long time, but the last couple of months they've fizzled out. **My nuclear power has disappeared. And I need to re-run my batteries.**"*

Daniel's verdict on why bid caps failed as the stable layer:

> "Bid caps, I mean, bid caps, they are a difficult technology to use specifically for, like, for this, like, base level, because **by design they are supposed to be volatile**."

### Learning phase

**Gradual drift gets learning-phase amnesty. A step change does not.**

> "during the week, as it was gradually getting more expensive, I thought, **okay, it's fine. It's the new campaigns, new audiences, lots of learning phase stuff.** But then **Saturday was insane.**" — Steven, 2026-06-23

> "sometimes those rules, when the new campaign starts, don't apply very well to begin with. **As it starts to learn.** And there might be a few days where we get a whole load of younger people that we don't want, but it should then learn that it needs to adjust to these older ones." — Steven, 2026-07-21

House-wide, the formal concept barely appears. The functional substitutes are:
1. **Minimum-spend floors per ad set** (€50/day).
2. **The 10× target CPA spend gate.**
3. **Buying algorithmic competence with volume:** *"we're giving them room to breathe… we started pushing ad spend into a campaign to generate data and then once the algorithm generated a certain amount of data, then it started making better decisions."* (Daniel, 2026-06-17)
4. **Algorithm re-orientation after geo exclusions:** *"the algorithm kind of needs to orient itself and take some time."* (Adam de Jong, 2026-06-23)
5. **Avoid re-entering ad review when changing targeting:** duplicate the ad set rather than edit targeting on a live one. *"Let's try and duplicate first, see what happens. If things go through great. If not, we change the targeting."* Nina's risk note: *"if things won't go through we will probably get the whole account rejected at this point."* (2026-06-26)

### Kill fast — but validate tracking first

The clearest kill quote in the corpus, on the health-insurer landing pages:

> "da haben wir eigentlich **direkt die Reißleine gezogen** und gesagt, okay, **das macht jetzt eigentlich keinen Sinn, da noch länger zu warten und mehr Daten zu ziehen**." — Daniel, Audibene SEA, 2026-06-01
> *(we pulled the ripcord immediately and said, ok, there's no point waiting any longer and pulling more data.)*

**But note the precondition stated immediately before it:** *"du hast ja validiert mit Dmitro, dass ja auch diese Conversion, die zwei auch da gesehen wurden"* (you validated with Dmytro that those conversions were actually being seen).

**Rule: validate tracking → then kill fast. Do not "collect more data" on a validated loser.**

### Protect the client's downside proactively

> "us as media buyers, we then need to be on top of this. And **if, for example, we see that things aren't performing, rather than keeping them running, being proactive about this pinging John saying like, hey John, this isn't working. We need to protect your downside.**" — Daniel, bi-weekly, 2026-06-26 (JVA, £100 CPA on a £49 course)

### Scale — and by how much

- **SEA: +5% step (€50 → €52.50), only on campaigns already under target on the 30-day average.**
- **Stop condition is CR2, not CPL.** Michelle: *"müssen dann halt weiterhin die CO2 [CR2] im Auge behalten, weil, wie du sagst, wenn die sich nicht verbessert und wir einfach immer mehr zahlen, dann wird es einfach irgendwann auch unnatürlich teuer. Aber **lass es uns machen und gucken und dann falls es halt reagieren**."* → **ship the increase, monitor CR2, revert if CR2 doesn't follow.**
- **Meta side, house-wide: ~20%/day.** Cost caps: *"can you please push the cost caps by 20% per day until they start bottoming out?"* (Daniel, 2026-06-01). Bid caps to unlock spend: *"I increased every bit cap by 20%, 10% for U.S. and Canada."* (Nina, 2026-07-14). Budgets: *"we would do a series of days where we increase the budget by 20%… take a week to get our budget up to €2,000 per day."* (Daniel, 2026-07-23)
- **If the cap is not being hit, do nothing.** *"Are we hitting 380 per day? We are not hitting 380 per day. Okay, so they are still fine… which means that I wouldn't necessarily do anything about them."* (Daniel, 2026-06-01)
- **Hitting the daily cap is itself an alert condition.** *"when we are hitting our daily limits on cost or bit caps, then this needs to be flagged to us so that we can review. So either we increase the spend or we drop the caps a little bit more."* (Daniel, 2026-06-01)
- **To throttle down, cut bid caps first, touch nothing else.** *"the first thing I would suggest is just decreasing some bit caps a bit. I wouldn't touch any other budget."* (Nina, 2026-07-21)
- **Keep-but-cap is a valid verdict.** *"logically this ad should probably be ad number three or four and it should probably stay there... for a while and have slightly lower spend than this. Try to get this like the CPA to like about 600."* (Kousha, 2026-05-05)
- **The agency's standing bias is that they under-scale.** *"this is generally something that I've noticed with Nina that sometimes she hesitates with ad spend increases… Because this is making or breaking the business."* (Daniel, AM Weekly, 2026-06-24). Nina's counter-position is recorded under *Disagreements*.

## 5. Structure conventions

### CBO vs ABO
